# Product

> Generated by `project-starter-pack`. This is the source of truth for **who this product is for, what it is, and why it exists**. Every other brief (design, technical) and every AI agent reading this repo should anchor here first.

## Register

Hybrid, leaning product. The marketing surface (homepage, pricing) does the brand work; the in-app surface earns familiarity through repeated use. When the two pull, the product side wins.

## One-liner

Bramble runs better engineering retros — the writing happens before the meeting, the meeting decides what changes, and every action item has an owner by the time the call ends.

## Users

### Primary

Engineering managers at Series A–C startups (20–200 engineers). They've been running biweekly retros for two years; the retros decayed into status meetings; their team stopped writing items because nothing changes. They've tried EasyRetro and a Notion template. They want something opinionated enough to push back when the meeting is going off the rails.

### Secondary

Tech leads and senior ICs who run retros for sub-teams (squads, pods). They care less about the brand and more about whether the tool gets out of their way.

### Jobs-to-be-done

- **Before the meeting**: capture what happened (good and bad) without typing in front of teammates.
- **During the meeting**: cluster, vote, decide — without losing the threads.
- **After the meeting**: walk out with owned action items that show up in the next retro pre-filled with their status.

## Product Purpose

Most retros fail at the hand-off, not the discussion. Items get raised, the team agrees something should change, and then nothing does — because nobody owned it, the action item lived in the meeting notes, and the meeting notes lived in a Confluence page nobody reopens. Bramble owns the hand-off: every action gets an owner and a due date before the meeting ends, carries forward into the next retro, and surfaces in the dashboard until it's resolved.

### Success metrics

- **Action-item completion rate** ≥60% by the next retro (vs. industry-typical 25%). This is the headline.
- **Pre-meeting writing rate** ≥75% of team members contribute at least one card before the meeting starts.
- **Median time-to-first-value** ≤8 minutes from sign-up to first card written.
- **30-day retention** of teams: ≥70%. A retro tool that isn't used again next sprint isn't a retro tool.

## Brand Personality

**Three words:** candid, structured, low-key.

We sound like a senior IC writing the agenda — direct, allergic to jargon, not trying to be funny. Sentences are short. Headlines state a fact, not a promise. We never use "delight", "supercharge", "unlock", or "platform".

## Anti-references

- **EasyRetro**: column-of-stickies UI, no opinion about the meeting itself, no follow-through.
- **Confluence "Team retrospective" template**: a blank page that pretends to be a tool.
- **Miro / FigJam**: infinite-canvas tools that turn every retro into a 45-minute setup tax.
- **Notion**: a database tool dressed as a meeting tool.
- **Slack stand-up bots that pretend to be retros** (Geekbot, Standuply): async messaging is not facilitation.
- **Visual register to avoid**: rounded purple gradients, hero-metric homepages ("10× better retros"), illustrated mascots, the "trusted by" logo strip at 40% opacity.

## Design Principles

1. **The meeting is the product.** The pre- and post-meeting surfaces exist to make the meeting better, not to replace it.
2. **Opinions over options.** When there's a right way, ship it. Don't expose a setting.
3. **Writing before talking.** Every flow assumes the team types before they speak.
4. **Owners or it didn't happen.** No action item exists without a name and a date.
5. **Carry the state forward.** The next retro starts with last retro's open items already on the screen.
6. **Quiet defaults.** No celebratory animations, no streak counters, no leaderboards.

## Accessibility & Inclusion commitments

WCAG 2.1 AA throughout, with two project-specific commitments above baseline:
- **Keyboard-only retro facilitation must be possible end-to-end.** A facilitator using a screen reader should be able to run the meeting from start to finish without a mouse.
- **Reduced-motion respected on every surface.** No exceptions for "delight" moments — there are none.
