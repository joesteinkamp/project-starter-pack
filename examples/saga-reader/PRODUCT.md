# Product

> Generated by `project-starter-pack`. This is the source of truth for **who this product is for, what it is, and why it exists**. Every other brief (design, technical) and every AI agent reading this repo should anchor here first.

## Register

product — a daily-use reading tool where earned familiarity beats novelty. The interface should disappear into the reading.

## One-liner

Saga is a reading app for long-form nonfiction that remembers where your attention went, not just where you stopped.

## Users

### Primary

Committed nonfiction readers — people who finish 15-40 books and a steady diet of long essays a year, mostly on a tablet or phone in the evening. They have tried Kindle (too closed, annotations are hostage) and read-it-later apps like Pocket and Instapaper (great for saving, weak for *finishing* and for revisiting what they marked). They keep a notes file or a Notion page of quotes by hand and resent the copy-paste tax.

### Secondary

Researchers and writers who read to extract — they need their highlights to leave the app cleanly (Markdown, not a walled garden) and to find a passage months later by what it was about, not by which book it was in.

### Jobs-to-be-done

- **Finish** the long thing I started, across devices, without losing my place or my momentum.
- **Capture** a passage in one gesture and trust I can find it again.
- **Return** to what I marked and actually do something with it (review, export, connect).

## Product Purpose

Most reading apps optimize for *saving* and for *page count*. The hard problem for a committed reader isn't storing more — it's finishing what matters and getting value back out of the marking they already do. Saga treats highlights and the re-reading of them as the core loop, not a side feature. We win when people finish more of what they start and revisit their own notes weeks later.

### Success metrics

- **Completion rate:** % of started long-reads finished within 30 days (target: lift over a user's pre-Saga baseline, self-reported at onboarding).
- **Highlight-return rate:** % of highlights revisited at least once after creation (north-star; target ≥ 35% at 30 days).
- **Time-to-first-value:** import a book and make a first highlight in under 3 minutes.
- **7-day and 30-day retention** of readers who completed onboarding.

## Brand Personality

**Three words:** literary, unobtrusive, exacting

Saga sounds like a well-edited book, not a productivity coach. Copy is plain and declarative — "Imported. Pick up on page 42." not "🎉 Success! Your book is ready to enjoy!". It never gamifies reading with streaks or confetti; the reward is the reading itself. When it speaks, it is precise: it names the exact chapter, the exact passage, the exact next step.

## Anti-references

- **Kindle** — closed annotation model, cramped typography controls, your highlights are hostage.
- **Goodreads** — social-feed-first, reading as performance and shelf-display.
- **Medium** — claps, member walls, algorithmic homepage; reading as a metrics surface.
- **Duolingo-style gamification** — streaks, badges, confetti, loss-aversion nudges.
- **Generic "AI reading assistant" landing pages** — purple gradients, "summarize any book in seconds," chat-bubble heroes.

## Design Principles

- **The text is the interface.** Chrome recedes; typography and the page do the work.
- **One gesture to capture, zero friction to return.** Marking and revisiting are the product, so they must be the cheapest actions.
- **Your library is yours.** Everything exports as clean Markdown; no lock-in, no copy-paste tax.
- **Calm over engagement.** No streaks, no badges, no notifications that manufacture urgency.
- **Show the exact place.** Always name the real location (chapter, page, passage) rather than a vague "continue."
- **Earned familiarity.** The layout a reader learns on night one is the layout on night three hundred.

## Accessibility & Inclusion commitments

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline, with reading-specific commitments above it: user-controlled type size, line-height, and measure; a true dark theme tuned for low-light evening reading (not inverted gray); full keyboard reachability and visible focus for every control; `prefers-reduced-motion` respected on every page turn and transition; screen-reader-labelled highlights and navigation. Reading-level of product copy targets 8th grade.

## Anti-patterns (always reject)

- Vague personas that could describe ten million people.
- Generic positioning that would fit a competitor's site.
- Education framing for an audience that already knows the problem.
- Hedging language ("might", "could", "consider") in brand voice.
- Feature lists standing in for positioning.
- Ornamental metrics ("engagement") instead of real ones.
- Anti-references that aren't real, named things.
- Brand personality of three interchangeable buzzwords.
