# Writing

> Generated by `project-starter-pack` from `PRODUCT.md` and `DESIGN.md` at orchestrate time. This file owns how words get written in this repository, from a button label to a page of prose. Personality and register are decided in `PRODUCT.md`; this file turns them into execution rules. Edit the briefs and re-run `/starter:orchestrate` rather than hand-editing this one.

## Voice

Saga sounds like a well-edited book, never a productivity coach (`PRODUCT.md`: literary, unobtrusive, exacting). As writing directives:

- Plain and declarative. "Imported. Pick up on page 42." One idea per sentence.
- Second person for instructions; no person at all for status. The app states facts. It does not cheer, apologize twice, or celebrate.
- Exact over vague. Name the chapter, the page, the passage, the next step. "Continue" always carries the place: "Continue — *Title*, p.42".
- Calm at all times. No urgency the reader didn't create, no streak language, no exclamation points.
- Wit is not part of this voice. Where other products joke in empty states, Saga is simply clear.

## Vocabulary & terminology

- Use the reader's words, exactly as their mental model has them: **book**, **chapter**, **page**, **highlight**, **note**, **tag**. Never "session", "annotation", "annotation object", "resume token", "content", "asset", "item".
- Casing: sentence case everywhere, including buttons and headings. Product areas are capitalized as proper names: Library, Reader, Marks.
- "Import" is the word for bringing a book in (never "upload"). "Export" always leaves as Markdown (never "download your data").
- Reading level: 8th grade for all product copy (per the `PRODUCT.md` accessibility commitment). Short words carry the meaning.

## Microcopy

- Buttons name the specific act: "Import a book", "Add note", "Export as Markdown", "Retry the rest". Never "Submit", "OK", or a bare "Continue" without the place.
- Errors say what happened and what to do next, and name the thing: "This PDF has no selectable text — OCR isn't supported yet. Try the EPUB instead." Never "Oops" or "Something went wrong."
- Destructive actions get an inline undo, and the toast states the fact: "Deleted · Undo". No "Are you sure?" copy.
- Empty states are one heading, one sentence, one action: "No books yet — import your first."
- Loading and progress name the stage ("Parsing chapter 6 of 14"), not a spinner adjective ("Working our magic").
- Locations are written exactly and consistently: "p.42, *Chapter 3*".

## Long-form

Onboarding, release notes, and docs keep the same register at a longer measure:

- Lead with the point; the first sentence says what changed or what to do.
- Sentences stay under about 20 words, one idea each. Connected thoughts are a paragraph, not a bullet list.
- Evidence is concrete: "imports a 600-page EPUB in under 10 seconds", not "blazing fast". Sources are named or the claim is cut.
- No launch hype ("game changer", "we're thrilled"). State what the reader can now do and where to find it.
- The voice may stretch to warmth in release notes, never to enthusiasm punctuation or emoji.

## Writing anti-patterns to avoid

- AI-flagship words (delve, leverage, seamless, robust, tapestry, elevate, "game changer", "paradigm shift"); empty adverbs; filler phrases ("it's worth noting", "let's dive in").
- Binary contrasts ("It's not X. It's Y."), negative listing, colon reveals, dramatic fragmentation, robotic rhythm, synonym cycling.
- Throat-clearing and faux-insight openers ("Here's the thing", "what nobody tells you"); rhetorical setups; weasel attribution ("studies show") without a named source.
- Importance puffery ("pivotal moment"), fake-strong verbs ("serves as a hub"), trailing analysis clauses ("…, highlighting our commitment").
- Summary-recap endings ("In conclusion") and fake-profound kickers; end on the concrete point.
- Formatting slop: emoji in headings, decorative bold, bullets where prose reads better, headers over tiny sections, em-dash clusters.
- Microcopy slop: cutesy errors ("Oops!"), blame-the-user copy, exclamation-point enthusiasm, hedging labels, vague button verbs ("Submit", "OK").

> Slop test: if a reader who has skimmed a thousand AI-written pages would say "yep, AI wrote this," find the tell, kill it, ship.
