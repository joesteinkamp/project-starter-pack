# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Critique the work, not the person. Assume the other person is trying.

## Expected behavior

- Give feedback on the change, with reasons. "This breaks the portability contract because Codex
  has no command surface" is useful; "this is wrong" is not.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a change that is good work but a poor fit, and say so
  without litigating it further.
- Respect that contributors differ in experience, native language, tooling, and time available.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, pull requests, or commit messages.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled decisions to wear people down, or dismissing a
  contributor rather than their argument.

## Scope

This applies in every space this project uses — issues, pull requests, discussions, commits, and
code review — and when someone represents the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report conduct issues to the maintainer through GitHub's
[private vulnerability reporting](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/security-advisories/guidance-on-reporting-and-writing/privately-reporting-a-security-vulnerability)
on this repository, which is private and does not require disclosing a personal email address, or
by opening an issue if the matter is already public.

Reports are read and answered. Responses range from a private word to a correction on the thread
to a block, depending on severity and pattern. The maintainer decides, and will say what was
decided to the person who reported it.

This document is deliberately short and locally enforced rather than adopting a longer external
covenant, because a one-maintainer project cannot honestly promise a committee process it does not
have.
