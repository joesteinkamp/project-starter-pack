# Product Anti-patterns

These are the product-positioning mistakes that quietly poison everything downstream. The starter-pack rejects them by default; if a project has a deliberate reason to override one, document the reason in `PRODUCT.md`.

## PRD-01 — Vague personas

> "Developers who want better tools."

A persona that could describe ten million people doesn't help anyone make a decision. Specify role, seniority, company stage, environment, what they tried last, what made them stop.

## PRD-02 — Generic positioning

> "The all-in-one platform for modern teams."

If the same sentence could appear on a competitor's site, it's not positioning. Anchor on the specific job, the specific user, the specific moment.

## PRD-03 — Education framing for an aware audience

If users already know they have the problem, don't teach them they have it. Solve it.

## PRD-04 — Hedging language in brand voice

"Might", "could", "consider", "perhaps". Brands that hedge in their own voice train AI to hedge in their UI.

## PRD-05 — Feature lists as positioning

A bulleted list of features tells the user nothing about whether the product is for them. Lead with the promise, support with proof.

## PRD-06 — Ornamental success metrics

"Increased engagement" is not a metric. "7-day retention", "task success rate", "median time to first value" are metrics. Measure what you'd be embarrassed to miss.

## PRD-07 — Anti-references that aren't real

If the anti-references in `PRODUCT.md` are abstract ("not boring", "not corporate"), they don't constrain anything. Name actual products, actual sites, actual aesthetics to avoid.

## PRD-08 — Brand personality of three buzzwords

"Modern, clean, friendly" describes 90% of products. The personality should be specific enough that you could recognize it in a paragraph of copy with the logo removed.
